# Notes: The Fight Over Open Source AI, Anthropic's $1.5B Payout, NYC Socialists: Evictions = Violence?

## Summary

The episode's central conclusion is that proposed bans on Chinese open-source AI are **regulatory capture**, not national security policy.

Kimi K3's release rattled US AI labs enough that Anthropic began lobbying to restrict American access to Chinese open-source models. The panel dismantles this framing quickly. If Anthropic's real concern were "distillation attacks" — competitors training on their outputs — they would push to ban Chinese access to American models, not the reverse. The actual motive is protecting duopoly valuations during a fundraising roadshow.

The hypocrisy runs deeper. Anthropic and OpenAI trained on unlicensed content themselves, which is why neither will publicly call China's behaviour IP theft. Doing so would torpedo their own fair-use defence in live litigation. The panel's shorthand: "IP for we, but not for thee."

The underlying economics make the lobby effort futile anyway. The **model layer** is commoditising fast — 95% of tasks run fine on cheaper, open-source alternatives, and startups are already migrating. Terminal value sits in applications above and cloud or chip infrastructure below. Google's CapEx surge, judged against its 32% average ROIC over 20 years, looks like exactly that kind of durable infrastructure bet — a buy signal, not a warning.

The final segment turns to New York City, where a push to ban credit checks, freeze rents, and block evictions is framed by some councillors as preventing "violence." The panel's response is blunt: removing landlords' ability to screen tenants forces them to price default risk upfront, rents rise, supply shrinks, and ghost apartments multiply. Property rights, they argue, are the foundation the rest of the argument rests on.
