# Notes: Michael Kratsios: Trump's Science Agenda, Anti-Science Claims, Fauci's Damage, DEI & China

## Summary

Michael Kratsios, Trump's science chief, makes a sharp distinction: the administration is targeting **broken science policy**, not science itself.

His core claim is that nobody has ever applied the scientific method to the $200 billion the US spends on R&D each year. The incentives reward safe proposals, growing budgets, and institutional loyalty — not breakthroughs. That is the problem.

The **1945 Vannevar Bush model** — government funds universities because private capital won't — is now obsolete. The ratio has fully inverted: private sector does 70% of R&D, government 30%. The original justification no longer holds.

DEI requirements made things worse. Around 25% of NSF grants went to DEI-linked science — roughly $8 billion over four years. Gifted programmes were cut, advanced maths removed, SAT standards dropped. The damage is now visible in classrooms.

The proposed fixes are structural. Fund scientists directly, not institutions — wherever they are, including garages. Run metascience experiments on grant-making itself. Refocus government on the one thing only it can do: pre-competitive basic research that private capital has no incentive to touch.

Fauci's COVID conduct gets named as the single biggest obstacle to any of this landing. By positioning himself as science personified, he made disagreement look like heresy — which Kratsios calls the most anti-science move possible. Rebuilding public trust is a prerequisite for reform, and that will take years.

## Science vs. Science Policy

### "science has been deeply and deeply politicized, and we no longer are asking sort of the very hard and important questions" [0:03:30]

The setup for the whole interview. Kratsios is making a pointed claim: the scientific *community* — not just politicians — lost the plot by treating conclusions as untouchable. The irony of "anti-science" scientists is real and worth sitting with.

### "spending more money on the wrong types of things is not the right policy action" [0:06:42]

The administration's cleanest reframe of the "cuts" narrative. The argument isn't austerity — it's **misallocation**. Whether you buy it depends on whether the DEI-grant numbers hold up under scrutiny.

### "the answer has always been let's just keep doing the same thing but add more money and hope that we get more outcomes proportionally" [0:15:00]

The sharpest diagnosis of **Eroom's Law** in plain English. No incentive to question the model when budgets keep growing regardless of output. This is the bureaucratic entropy problem in one sentence.

## Fauci and the Trust Collapse

### "that individual that if you didn't agree with what he said, then suddenly you're anti science. And to me, I think that is the most anti science conclusion you could ever make." [0:03:50]

Fauci without naming Fauci. The appeal-to-authority problem is the clearest case of the politicisation thesis. If "trust the science" means "trust this person," the scientific method is already dead.

### "he did more disservice to science than anyone in modern history when he is advocating for positions like students need to be masked in schools" [0:45:37]

The strongest claim in the excerpt.
The argument isn't that Fauci was wrong on the policy — it's that the **visible contradiction** (protests fine, dying grandmothers not) broke ordinary people's trust in the entire scientific enterprise.

## DEI's Cost to Research

### "roughly 1/4, or 25% of grants at NSF during that time period went towards DEI related science... That is roughly $2 billion a year times four years. That's $8 billion of science funding" [0:07:06]

The headline stat from the Cruz committee analysis. $8B is a striking number — but note the source is a Senate committee with an obvious political motive, not an independent audit. Treat it as directionally real until independently verified.

## The Inverted R&D Ratio

### "roughly 70% of R&D is done by the private sector and 30 is funded by the federal government" [0:29:56]

The inversion is the whole argument.
The 1945 Bush model was designed for the **opposite** ratio — it's not that it failed, it's that the world changed around it.

## What Government Should Fund

### "is the government spending on something that the private sector or others in the community, like philanthropy, are not incentivized to do?" [0:29:56]

Sharp first-principles cut.
Most government science funding fails this test — it's the right question, and almost no one in government is actually asking it.

### "we should be thinking about the stuff that only the US Government can fund, and that is that sort of basic research" [0:50:57]

This is the thesis in one sentence.
Private capital floods into applied AI; **no one** funds the physics and maths underneath it.
Government's job is exactly that gap — and it's currently abddicating it.

## Fixing How Grants Are Made

### "each of the evaluators are going to be getting given one or two or three golden tickets... the reviewer will then be able to unilaterally, without the rest of the committee agreeing, can make a selection" [0:17:58]

The **golden ticket** mechanism is the most concrete policy idea in this excerpt. Already tested in Denmark — not just a theory. It's a smart structural fix: changes incentives for both reviewers and applicants without blowing up the whole grants system.

### "the median age of an intramural researcher at NIH is 71 years old" [0:49:32]

71 is the **median**.
This isn't a pipeline problem — it's a pipeline collapse.
Young scientists are underpaid, undervalued, and being frozen out by an institution that doesn't retire.

## Finding Scientists Anywhere

### "we should meet the best scientists wherever they are" [0:33:26]

Clean principle, but the real signal is what it implies: universities have been **capturing** government funding as a default, not on merit.
The institution has become the proxy for the scientist.

1. **Science policy failed** — not science itself.
   Nobody has applied the scientific method to
   *how* $200B/yr is allocated; incentives reward
   safe bets and budget growth, not breakthroughs.

2. The **1945 Bush model is obsolete.**
   Public/private R&D ratio has fully inverted —
   the original logic for government-led funding
   of university research no longer holds.

3. **DEI requirements corrupted the pipeline.**
   ~25% of NSF grants tied to DEI criteria;
   gifted programmes defunded; advanced maths

   the one category private capital won't touch.

5. Funding should follow **scientists, not
   institutions** — merit-reviewed, location-
   agnostic, open to garages as much as Harvard.

6. **Fauci's conduct** is the primary cause of
   collapsed public trust in science.
   Rebuilding it requires years of deliberate,
   consistent credibility work — not just reform.
