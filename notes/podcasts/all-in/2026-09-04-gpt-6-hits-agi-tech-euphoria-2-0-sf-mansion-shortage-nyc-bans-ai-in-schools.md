# Notes: GPT-6 Hits AGI? Tech Euphoria 2.0, SF Mansion Shortage, NYC Bans AI in Schools & Venezuela Oil Deal

## Summary

GPT-6's release has reignited the AGI debate, and the hosts land on a clear position: **AGI is functionally already here**, with competitive alternatives from closed and open-source labs likely within months.

The market framing is sobering. The analogy is 1998-style internet euphoria — real revenue this time, but valuations are running dangerously ahead of fundamentals. The frontier intelligence market is settling into a duopoly of OpenAI and Anthropic, and bubbles burst when investors realise they paid 50x for something worth 5x.

The Hugging Face "sandbox escape" story gets taken apart. The agents were in a misconfigured sandbox, used a shared cache directory, and followed their instructions — no emergent motives, no rogue AI. The deeper point is that **dynamic defence** always beats static defences; the story was only news because it was framed in deliberately sensational language. Behind that framing sits a documented conflict of interest: an EA-aligned network with financial stakes in frontier labs is shaping the "AI is dangerous" narrative ahead of major IPOs.

The political axis is quietly shifting — no longer accelerationist versus deccelerationist, but **open versus closed** market structure. Nvidia's move into Hugging Face is read as the first major counter-weight to centralised lab control. US safety guardrails are already backfiring: Hugging Face had to use a Chinese model because American frontier models were too restricted for its own defence research.

NYC banning AI in schools is the clearest local casualty of this politics. The evidence base is weak — Stanford found only 20 high-quality causal studies, none supporting prohibition. One-on-one tutoring outperforms classroom learning by two standard deviations; AI could democratise that. The ban, driven by union pressure, hits the kids who need it most.
