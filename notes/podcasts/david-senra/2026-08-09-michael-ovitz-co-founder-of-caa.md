# Notes: Michael Ovitz, Co-founder of CAA

## Summary

Michael Ovitz, now in his 70s, believes AI makes today more exciting than building CAA into Hollywood's dominant agency.

The reason is specific: until AI, everyone had to be a specialist. AI removed that constraint and handed the advantage to **generalists** — people who can get fluent in a new domain fast and move across problems freely. Ovitz sees himself as exactly that kind of operator, and it's why he feels the urgency of someone decades younger.

The playbook he ran at CAA hasn't changed. Talent first, then team, then financing, distribution, marketing, and culture — in that order, every time, every industry. Ego doesn't fit anywhere in that sequence. He borrowed Magic Johnson's fast-break model: don't care who scores, just win. When something didn't get done, he did it himself.

Two things defined how CAA competed. First, relentless outworking — he personally mailed torn-out ads to rivals every time CAA signed a star, for ten years straight. Second, **protecting vision** — what looks like stubbornness in high performers is usually signal preservation, not arrogance. He respects people who hold their ground until proven wrong.

His current bet sits underneath all of that. The Universal Music bid with Bill Ackman is a conviction play: music IP is undervalued infrastructure sitting below the AI content layer, and the market hasn't priced it correctly yet. Music doesn't die country by country — it's global cultural infrastructure. Owning it, he thinks, becomes the next major value unlock.

## AI Rewires the Generalist Advantage

### "I said to one of my young mentees, I said, consider me Dracula for the brain." [0:01:05]

Ovitz frames lifelong learning as a predatory act — **extracting** youth and capability from smart people around him. It's a better mental model than "staying curious." Curiosity is passive; this is acquisitive.

### "We all had to be specialists until AI. Now we're all generalists." [0:08:44]

He's operating across consumer products, high-tech, defence, medicine, biopharma — fields he's "totally unqualified for" — because AI lets him acquire just enough **vocabulary** to work alongside deep experts. The leverage goes to connectors and orchestrators, not subject-matter specialists. That's worth sitting with.

### "If I take a day off now, I feel like I've missed three months." [0:05:22]

Max Levchin's line, originally "missed a week," had already **upgraded to three months** by the time Ovitz saw him at the Allen Conference. The compression rate itself is accelerating. That's the uncomfortable part of the quote — it's not hyperbole, it's a data point with a trajectory.

## Outworking the Competition

### "I just know I'm going to outwork every one of them." [0:31:32]

When a close colleague at William Morris told him CAA would fail within a year, Ovitz didn't argue strategy — he went straight to **work ethic** as the decisive variable. No grand theory. Just a conviction that effort compounds faster than circumstance.

### "Every time we signed a movie star, I took that ad, tore it, put in an envelope to those four guys, and I did it for the next 10 years." [0:31:32]

Four partners told him CAA would **never** sign movie stars. He turned every signing into a weekly reminder — a red full-page Variety ad, torn out, mailed to the doubters, for a decade. That's not petty — that's using mockery as rocket fuel. Highly deliberate.

### "We're trying to blow out the competition as fast, as cleanly, as simply and as brutally as we can." [0:33:54]

He reframes competition as internal — "you're competing with yourself" — and enforces that via radical **time discipline**: 20-minute Zooms, no small talk, nothing padded to fill an hour. The brutality is directed inward first. That's what makes the external version credible.

## Defending Vision and Vision-Holders

### "I think they are protecting their vision. I think filmmakers have a very clear vision of what they want to achieve." [0:38:03]

The simplest explanation for why great creatives seem "difficult." This reframes every founder stubbornness story — it's not ego, it's signal fidelity. Worth holding next to the Loom curation logic: what looks like obstinacy is often just **noise rejection**.

### "I love people that hold their ground until you prove them wrong." [0:42:46]

Ovitz singles this out as what makes Josh Kushner exceptional — yielding a position when actually persuaded, not when pressured. This is the distinction most people miss. **Conviction plus updating** is rare; most people do one or the other.

## Building an Idea System

### "No idea is stupid because a bad idea may stimulate a good idea." [0:44:47]

Ovitz's operating thesis at CAA: take ideas from anybody, at any level. The circle-of-chairs ritual enforced this structurally — every 90 days, 200 people pitched with no opt-out, and a mailroom guy's cable idea got him promoted on the spot. The **mechanism** is the point — good culture without a forcing function is just vibes.

### "He won, we lost. Next case." [0:47:08]

Barry Diller's response to losing a major acquisition bid — six words, no dwelling. Ovitz says he still uses it. The lesson isn't detachment — it's that **rearward attention has zero ROI** once the outcome is fixed.

## Talent at the Top of Every Company

### "If you don't have someone who understands talent running the company... that company is going to die." [0:49:22]

Ovitz's thesis on why operators alone aren't enough — you can always hire an operator, but talent intuition can't be bolted on. This is his justification for the Universal Music bid and for backing Lucian Grange specifically. **Operator vs. talent-native leader** is a real distinction most cap tables ignore.

## Music IP as AI-Era Infrastructure

### "Music is never going away... it's part of our culture worldwide, not country by country." [0:53:08]

Ovitz's bull case for music IP: it underlies games, film, TV, AI, and every piece of user-generated content — and British/American music is a **universal cultural solvent** that crosses language barriers. This is a stronger moat argument than pure catalogue size. Worth thinking about in the context of any AI-layer business that needs a defensible content substrate.
