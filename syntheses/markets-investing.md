# Leverage, Valuation, and When Markets Punish You

Across these sources, a consistent warning emerges: markets are unforgiving when fundamentals are ignored or leverage is misused. The bluntest version is that leverage is the singular mechanism by which intelligent people destroy themselves. Paul Tudor Jones adds a structural layer — buying the S&P at current valuations has historically produced negative ten-year forward returns, making high prices the slow-motion version of the same trap. Dan Loeb's short-selling examples reveal the flip side: even companies with genuine competitive moats look like obvious shorts until they aren't, and acting on that apparent safety has repeatedly ruined people. Bill Maris introduces an institutional angle — large fund math is structurally broken, making size itself a form of hidden leverage against returns. Together they argue that discipline around price, position sizing, and capital structure matters more than being right about the underlying idea.
