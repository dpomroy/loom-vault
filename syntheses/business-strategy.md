# Ownership Structure as Competitive Moat

These sources converge on a counterintuitive idea: the deepest competitive advantages are structural, not operational. Costco's 'scale economies shared' model locks in loyalty by making low prices a constitutional commitment rather than a tactic. Rolex's foundation structure eliminates quarterly pressure entirely, letting management play a decades-long game no public competitor can match. Ferrari weaponizes artificial scarcity — always one car short of demand — turning supply constraint into brand equity. Thiel frames this most abstractly: monopoly comes from solving a unique problem, not from outcompeting on the same terms. Where they differ is mechanism: Costco wins through volume discipline, Rolex through ownership privacy, Ferrari through deliberate restraint, Thiel through category creation.
