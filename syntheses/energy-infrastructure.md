# AI's Hidden Bottleneck: Power, Not Compute

There's broad agreement that physical infrastructure — not algorithms or chips — is the binding constraint on AI's growth. The natural gas analysis makes this most concrete: AI data centers and LNG exports will collectively outpace what the US can produce and deliver by 2028, a collision course that was already forming before AI demand arrived. The power markets perspective reinforces this, noting AI merely accelerates a tightness that was already structural. The data center framing adds a useful conceptual bridge — electricity in, intelligence out — making clear that AI is fundamentally an energy business. Where sources diverge is on urgency and agency: the policy angle treats regulatory obstruction as the acute threat, while the infrastructure angle treats supply physics as the slow-moving but unavoidable one.
