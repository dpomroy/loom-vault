# Where Great Ideas Come From: Curiosity, Problem Choice, Originality

Curiosity as both engine and navigation system is the central claim in 'How to Do Great Work' — it doesn't just motivate, it directs. This pairs tightly with the argument that problem selection matters more than problem solving, a point most creators underweight. Zelnick adds a structural reason why: data is inherently backward-looking, so purely analytical approaches can never produce genuine surprise, which is what hits require. Rubin's angle is different but complementary — his outsider ignorance let him stay true to the art form rather than optimizing toward convention. Together, these sources argue that originality isn't technique; it's a refusal to let existing frameworks define what's worth pursuing.
