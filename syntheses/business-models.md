# Structural Moats: Low Prices, IP Flywheels, and Monopoly Logic

These sources converge on a single underlying idea: durable businesses aren't built on tactics but on structural positions that compound over time. Costco's model is literally named 'scale economies shared with customers' — a self-reinforcing loop where lower prices drive volume, which funds lower prices still. Disney's IP flywheel operates by the same logic but in a different medium: exposure in one node (film) amplifies value in others (parks, merchandise). Thiel frames this most abstractly — monopoly is the goal, competition is the failure state. What separates Rolex is the precision of its positioning: not cheapest, not most exclusive, but optimally placed on the supply-demand curve where volume and prestige coexist. Each model is structurally distinct, but all resist imitation for the same reason — they were designed around a reinforcing architecture, not a single feature.
