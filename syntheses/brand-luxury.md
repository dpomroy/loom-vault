# Scarcity, Myth, and the Art-Commerce Marriage

These sources converge on a counterintuitive truth: the most enduring luxury brands succeed by restraining commerce, not maximizing it. Ferrari's explicit policy of delivering one car fewer than demand and Rolex's shift from product specs to identity signaling both show that scarcity is engineered, not accidental. Disney adds a third dimension — that the commercial engine must be married to genuine artistic ambition, not merely decorated with it. Where they differ is in origin: Ferrari's myth was built around a difficult, indifferent founder whose contempt for customers became an asset, while Disney's was built by a founder who desperately wanted to connect with audiences. The mechanism of aloofness versus intimacy produces the same premium outcome.
